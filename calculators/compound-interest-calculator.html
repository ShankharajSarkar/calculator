How Compounding Frequency Affects Returns

Compound interest is interest earned on top of previously accumulated interest. Increasing the frequency from Annually (1x) to Monthly (12x) or Daily (365x) lets your money start generating returns on newer interest balances earlier in the financial cycle.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the compound interest formula for different frequencies? ▼
The compound interest formula is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where P is principal, r is annual nominal rate, n is compounding frequency (365 for daily, 52 for weekly, 12 for monthly, 1 for yearly), and t is time in years.
What is the difference between APR and APY? ▼
APR is the stated nominal annual percentage rate, whereas APY (Annual Percentage Yield) reflects the effective return earned after accounting for intra-year compounding cycles. A 12% nominal rate compounded monthly yields an effective APY of about 12.68%.
Why does the frequency comparison table show different numbers than my main result? ▼
The comparison table isolates the effect of compounding frequency alone, using only your initial principal with no recurring deposits, so it can be compared like-for-like across frequencies. Your main result includes any recurring deposits you've added, which is why the two numbers differ when deposits are switched on.
What is the Rule of 72 in compound interest? ▼
The Rule of 72 is a quick mental math shortcut: divide 72 by your annual interest rate to find out approximately how many years it will take for your money to double (e.g., at 12% return, your money doubles in roughly 72 / 12 = 6 years).
