How the National Pension System (NPS) Works

The National Pension System (NPS) is a government-sponsored, voluntary, long-term retirement savings scheme managed by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA). It allows you to invest systematically into a diversified mix of Equity (E), Corporate Debt (C), and Government Securities (G) across your working life.

NPS Maturity & Annuity Rules (Updated December 2025)

PFRDA significantly revised the exit and withdrawal framework in December 2025. Upon reaching age 60 (or deferring up to 85):

  • Corpus ≤ ₹8 Lakh: Full 100% lump-sum withdrawal is allowed, with no mandatory annuity purchase, for both government and non-government subscribers.
  • ₹8 Lakh to ₹12 Lakh: Lump sum is capped at whichever is lower — ₹6 lakh or 80% of the corpus — with the balance going toward an annuity.
  • Above ₹12 Lakh (Non-Government): Up to 80% can be taken as lump sum, with a minimum 20% mandatory annuity.
  • Above ₹12 Lakh (Government Employees): The older rule still applies — 60% lump sum, minimum 40% mandatory annuity.
  • Tax-Free Cap: Regardless of how much you're allowed to withdraw as lump sum, only 60% of your total corpus is tax-free under Section 10(12A) — any amount withdrawn beyond that is taxed at your slab rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What changed in the December 2025 PFRDA withdrawal reform? ▼
Non-government subscribers now only need a minimum 20% annuity (down from 40%) and can take up to 80% as a lump sum. Government employees continue under the older 60% lump sum / 40% annuity rule. The deferral age was also raised from 75 to 85, and the full-withdrawal-without-annuity threshold was raised from Rs 5 lakh to Rs 8 lakh.
How much of my NPS lump sum is actually tax-free? ▼
Only 60% of your total corpus is tax-free under Section 10(12A), regardless of how much you're allowed to withdraw as a lump sum. If you use the new 80% lump sum option, the extra 20% above the 60% tax-free portion is taxable at your slab rate.
What is the extra ₹50,000 tax deduction under Section 80CCD(1B)? ▼
Section 80CCD(1B) provides an exclusive tax deduction of up to ₹50,000 for voluntary contributions to an NPS Tier 1 account. This deduction is over and above the ₹1.5 Lakh limit available under Section 80C/80CCD(1), allowing total tax deductions of up to ₹2 Lakh per financial year under the Old Tax Regime.
What happens in the event of the subscriber's demise before retirement? ▼
In the unfortunate event of the subscriber's demise, 100% of the accumulated NPS corpus is paid directly to the registered nominee or legal heir as a tax-free lump sum.
