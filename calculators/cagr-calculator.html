Understanding CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)

The Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) is the metric used by financial analysts and mutual fund managers to measure the constant rate of return on an investment assuming all profits are reinvested over multiple years.

CAGR vs Absolute Return: The Real Difference

If an investment of ₹1 Lakh grows to ₹2 Lakh over 5 years:

  • Absolute Return: $\frac{₹2,00,000 - ₹1,00,000}{₹1,00,000} \times 100 = \mathbf{100\%}$
  • CAGR (Annualized): $(\frac{₹2,00,000}{₹1,00,000})^{1/5} - 1 = \mathbf{14.87\% \text{ p.a.}}$
CAGR = (Final Value / Initial Value)(1 / Years) − 1

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Why is CAGR preferred over average annual returns? ▼
Average annual returns ignore the volatility and timing of compounding. CAGR smooths out fluctuating market returns (e.g., +40% in year one, -20% in year two) to give the true single-rate compound progression.
When is Absolute Return better than CAGR? ▼
Absolute return is best suited for investments held for less than 1 year. For time periods exceeding 1 year, CAGR is the industry standard benchmark.
What is a good CAGR return in the Indian Market? ▼
Historically in India, large-cap equity index funds (Nifty 50) have delivered 12% to 13% CAGR, mid/small-caps have generated 15% to 18% CAGR, and bank FDs deliver 6.5% to 7.5% CAGR over long 10+ year horizons.
